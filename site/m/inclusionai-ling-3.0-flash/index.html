Here's one that tends to surprise people:

Cleopatra lived closer in time to the Moon landing (1969) than to the building of the Great Pyramid of Giza. The Great Pyramid was completed around 2560 BCE — roughly 2,500 years before Cleopatra's reign (69–30 BCE). But the Moon landing was only about 2,000 years after she lived. So the distance of time between Cleopatra and us is shorter than the distance between Cleopatra and the ancient Egyptians who built the pyramids.

It feels wrong because we tend to picture Cleopatra as ancient beyond measure, but the pyramids are that much older than she was.

A couple more you might not know:

  • Honey never spoils. Archaeologists have found 3,000-year-old honey in Egyptian tombs that was still perfectly edible. Its low moisture, acidic pH, and natural hydrogen peroxide make it virtually immortal.

  • There's a biological immortal. Turritopsis dohrnii, a tiny species of jellyfish, can revert its cells back to their earliest form when stressed or injured, essentially restarting its life cycle indefinitely — making it biologically immortal.
