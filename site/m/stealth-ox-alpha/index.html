Here's one that tends to break people's brains:

Every time you thoroughly shuffle a deck of cards, you're almost certainly creating an order that has never existed before in human history.

The number of ways to arrange 52 cards is 52 factorial (52!), which works out to:

80,658,175,170,943,878,571,660,636,856,403,766,975,289,505,440,883,277,824,000,000,000,000

That's roughly 8 × 10⁶⁷ — a number so large it defies intuition. If every star in the observable universe had a billion planets, and every planet had a trillion people, and every person had shuffled a deck once per second since the Big Bang, you still wouldn't come close to exhausting all possible arrangements.

So the specific sequence sitting in your card drawer right now is, with near-certainty, unique in the entire history of the universe. Not bad for a cheap pack of playing cards.

Here's one that tends to break people's brains: a day on Venus lasts longer than a year on Venus.

Venus takes about 243 Earth days to rotate once on its axis, but only about 225 Earth days to complete an orbit around the Sun. So technically, its "day" outlasts its "year."

Bonus weirdness: Venus also rotates backwards compared to most planets, meaning if you stood on its surface, you'd see the Sun rise in the west and set in the east.
