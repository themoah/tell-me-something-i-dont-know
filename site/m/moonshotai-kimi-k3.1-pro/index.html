Here’s something you might not know: your brain sometimes backdates what you see.

When you glance quickly at a clock, its second hand can appear frozen for longer than a second. This is called chronostasis, or the stopped-clock illusion. While your eyes are moving, your brain suppresses the blurry visual input. Afterward, it appears to fill in that gap with the first clear image—making it seem as though the clock was already in your view during the eye movement.

In other words, your experience of “now” is partly a seamless reconstruction, not a direct live feed.
