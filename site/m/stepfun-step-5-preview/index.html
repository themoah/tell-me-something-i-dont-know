Here’s one: If two pieces of the same metal touch in the vacuum of space, they will permanently fuse together into a single piece.

This is called cold welding. On Earth, every metal surface is covered in a microscopic layer of oxidation and grime, so the atoms of two different pieces never actually touch. But in the vacuum of space, clean metal has no oxide layer—so if two bare surfaces press together, the atoms bond as if they were part of the same object all along.

It’s a real headache for engineers designing satellites and spacecraft, which is why space hardware is often coated with gold or special dry lubricants to keep parts from accidentally becoming one.
