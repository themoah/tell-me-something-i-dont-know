Here's one that tends to stop people in their tracks:

Honey is the only food that literally never goes bad. Archaeologists have found 3,000-year-old honey in Egyptian tombs — and it was still perfectly edible.

The secret is honey's unique chemistry:

  • Extremely low moisture content — it's hygroscopic but holds almost no free water, so bacteria can't grow in it.
  • Naturally acidic (pH ~3.9) — most pathogens can't survive in that environment.
  • Hydrogen peroxide production — an enzyme added by bees when they mix nectar creates a mild antiseptic.
  • A natural "open jar" preservative — its low water activity essentially dehydrates any microorganism that tries to invade.

The one catch? It can ferment if it absorbs too much moisture from the air, which is why keeping the lid sealed matters. But in sealed conditions? It's practically eternal.

So if you ever find an ancient jar of honey — don't worry, it's fine to eat. 😄
