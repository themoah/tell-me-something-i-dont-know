You spend part of every day effectively blind, and your brain edits it out.

Your eyes don't glide smoothly across the world — they jump between points in rapid movements called saccades, a few times every second. During each jump, your brain suppresses visual input so you don't see a smeared blur. Add it up and you're functionally blind for a significant chunk of every day without noticing.

Related trick: you'll never see your own eyes move in a mirror. Flick your gaze between your left and right eye — you'll see them in both positions but never in motion, while someone standing next to you can see the movement perfectly clearly.
