Here's one that genuinely surprised me:

Before mechanical clocks became widespread, an "hour" wasn't a fixed length of time.

In medieval Europe and ancient Rome, people divided daylight into 12 hours and nighttime into 12 hours. But daylight varies enormously with the seasons. So in summer, an "hour" might be 75 minutes, and in winter, it might be only 45 minutes.

A "summer hour" and a "winter hour" were both just called an hour — the same word, different duration. You could tell what season it was just by how long someone's "hour" took.

Clocks didn't just tell time — they redefined what time is. The invention of the mechanical clock essentially forced the world to agree that an hour is always exactly 60 minutes, regardless of whether the sun is up or down. That's a choice we made, not something nature gave us.

It's one of those facts that makes you realize how much of what feels "objective" is really just a convention we all silently agreed to.
