A typical fluffy cumulus cloud — the kind you'd draw as a kid — weighs about 1.1 million pounds (roughly 500,000 kg), or as much as 100 elephants.

It stays airborne because that water is split into billions of microscopic droplets spread across a huge volume, making the cloud slightly less dense than the air around it. So the sky is essentially holding up a herd of elephants over your head, and it looks like cotton.
