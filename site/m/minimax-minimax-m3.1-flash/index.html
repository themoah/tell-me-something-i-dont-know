Here are a few things that reliably surprise people:

Sharks existed before trees. Sharks showed up roughly 450 million years ago; the first forests didn't appear until ~390 million years ago. For about 60 million years, sharks were the apex predators of a planet that was mostly empty of land animals.

Wombats poop cubes. Actual cubic droppings. A 2018 study found the shape comes from the intestine's varying elasticity squeezing the feces into flat-sided molds as they dry — and it makes them stack, which is how wombats mark territory.

The first "computer bug" was literally a moth. On September 9, 1947, a moth got stuck in a relay of the Harvard Mark II. Grace Hopper's team taped it into the logbook with the note "First actual case of bug being found." The moth is now in the Smithsonian.

Octopuses edit their own RNA. They can rewrite messenger RNA on the fly, letting their cells adapt to changing conditions — a capability we only began to understand in 2013. They also have three hearts, copper-based blue blood, and taste with their suckers.

Oxford University is older than the Aztec Empire. Teaching was happening at Oxford by 1096. Tenochtitlan was founded in 1428.

The Eiffel Tower changes height with the weather. Iron expands in heat, so it grows about 15 cm taller in summer and shrinks in winter.

More possible chess games than atoms in the observable universe. The Shannon number (~10^120) for possible games dwarfs the ~10^80 atoms in the cosmos.

Bonus oddity: the inventor of the Pringles can, Fredric Beur, requested that half his ashes be buried in a can of Original flavor.

Here's one that usually gets people: sharks existed before trees.

Shark-like fish showed up roughly 450 million years ago, in the oceans of the Silurian. The first real forests — the towering Archaeopteris — wouldn't appear for another 70 million years or so. So for a stretch of Earth's history, the land was basically barren rock and fungus, and the ocean was already full of things that looked a lot like modern sharks.

  • Wombats produce cube-shaped poop. Australian researchers in 2018 worked out why: the intestines aren't uniform, so the final stretch of the gut squeezes the waste into flat-sided blocks as it dries. It's the only known animal that does it.

  • The Eiffel Tower grows about 15 cm taller in summer, because steel expands in heat. It's shrunk back to its winter size ever since.
  • The inventor of the Pringles can was buried in one — Fredric Baur requested that a portion of his ashes go in the original flavor, and his family obliged.
