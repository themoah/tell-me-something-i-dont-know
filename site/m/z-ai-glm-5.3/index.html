Here's one that surprises most people: honey never spoils. Archaeologists have found pots of honey in ancient Egyptian tombs that are over 3,000 years old and still perfectly edible. Honey's low moisture content, natural acidity, and the fact that bees add an enzyme that produces small amounts of hydrogen peroxide make it essentially inhospitable to bacteria and spoilage.

A couple more, in case that one's old news to you:

  • Octopuses have three hearts, and two of them stop beating when they swim, which is why they'd rather crawl — swimming exhausts them.
  • The Eiffel Tower can grow about 15 cm (6 inches) taller in summer because the metal expands in the heat.
  • Bananas are slightly radioactive — they contain potassium-40. Not enough to matter (you'd need millions at once for a dangerous dose), but it's a real effect, enough that "banana equivalent dose" is informally used to explain radiation levels.

I can't actually know what you already know, but here's a handful of facts that tend to surprise people:

The Moon smells like gunpowder. Apollo astronauts reported that lunar dust, which clung to their suits, had a distinctive odor similar to spent gunpowder or wet ashes. Nobody's entirely sure why, since the Moon's composition doesn't match gunpowder's ingredients.

Point Nemo exists. There's a spot in the Pacific Ocean so remote that the nearest humans are often astronauts aboard the International Space Station, which orbits about 400 km overhead. It's so far from land that it's used as a "spacecraft cemetery" — over 260 decommissioned satellites and vehicles have been deliberately crashed there.

Pigeons can read medical scans. In studies, trained pigeons identified cancerous tissue in mammogram and microscope images with accuracy rivaling human pathologists. Their visual pattern recognition is genuinely that good.

"Silly" used to be a compliment. The word comes from Old English sælig, meaning blessed or happy. Over centuries it drifted: blessed → innocent → harmless → weak → foolish. Linguists call this "semantic pejoration."

T. rex is closer to you than to Stegosaurus. About 83 million years separate Tyrannosaurus rex from the present day, but around 85–90 million years separate it from Stegosaurus. Stegosaurus was already ancient history when T. rex showed up.
