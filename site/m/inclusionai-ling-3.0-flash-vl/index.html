Here's one that might surprise you:

Tardigrades (nicknamed "water bears") can survive in the vacuum of outer space.

In 2007, scientists exposed tardigrades to the harsh conditions of space for 10 days aboard the FOTON-M3 mission — including direct exposure to solar UV radiation, near-absolute-zero temperatures, and a complete vacuum. When brought back to Earth, many of them were still alive and even went on to reproduce normally.

They can withstand:

  • Temperatures from −272°C (just above absolute zero) to over 150°C
  • Radiation doses 1,000 times what would kill a human
  • Pressures six times greater than the deepest point in the ocean
  • The complete vacuum of space

They do this by entering a state called cryptobiosis, essentially shutting down all metabolic processes and curling into a dried-out ball called a "tun." In this state, they can remain dormant for decades and revive when conditions improve.

Tardigrades are arguably the most resilient organisms known to science — and they're microscopic, typically about 0.5 mm long. 🐻💧

Here's one: Trees communicate with each other and share resources through an underground fungal network — sometimes called the "Wood Wide Web."

Through symbiotic mycorrhizal fungi that connect their root systems, trees can:

  • Transfer carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, and water to neighboring trees — even between different species.
  • Send chemical warning signals when under attack by insects or disease, prompting nearby trees to ramp up their own defenses.
  • Preferentially nourish their offspring — "mother trees" have been observed funneling significantly more nutrients to their seedlings through these underground connections.

The network can span vast areas. In British Columbia, a single old-growth Douglas fir was found to be connected to hundreds of neighboring trees through this fungal web. When that tree was dying, it actively dumped carbon and nutrients into the network — essentially redistributing its wealth to the community before it died.

So a forest isn't just a collection of competing individuals. It's more like a cooperative society, quietly sharing resources and information beneath our feet.
