Here's one that overturns a common assumption: the mantis shrimp has worse color vision than you do, despite having about a dozen types of color photoreceptor (you have three).

You'd expect more receptors to mean richer color perception, but experiments in 2014 found mantis shrimp are actually poor at telling apart similar colors — worse than humans, in fact. The reason seems to be that they skip the step we use. Vertebrates compare signals from different receptors against each other ("opponent processing"), which is computationally expensive but precise. Mantis shrimp instead appear to recognize colors more like a barcode: which receptor fires hardest, and by how much, is read as a pattern. It's a fast, low-effort system — probably a good trade for an animal that needs to make split-second decisions in a busy reef environment.

They do have abilities we lack, though: they can see circularly polarized light (as far as anyone knows, they're the only animal that can), and their eyes are split into three regions that each track motion independently.
