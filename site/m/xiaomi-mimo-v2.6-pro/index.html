Here's one that always blows my mind:

A brainless, single-celled slime mold can outperform humans at network design.

Physarum polycephalum — a slime mold with no brain, no neurons, and no central intelligence — can solve mazes, make trade-off decisions, and optimize complex networks. In a famous 2010 experiment by Japanese researchers, they placed food dots on a map of Tokyo in the positions of major cities and let slime mold grow between them. It reconstructed a network almost identical to the real Tokyo rail system — an engineering network designed by thousands of humans over decades.

It does this through a surprisingly elegant mechanism: as it explores, it thickens pathways that reach food and prunes the ones that don't, essentially running a decentralized optimization algorithm without any "thinking" at all.

It even appears to exhibit a form of memory — it remembers where it has already explored and avoids those areas, despite having no brain to store that information. The mechanism is thought to be encoded in the physical structure of its own body — essentially a living, soft-matter computer.

Researchers now use slime molds as inspiration for designing more efficient real-world transportation and communication networks.
