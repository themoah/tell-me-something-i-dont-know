Here's one: Sloths can hold their breath longer than dolphins.

A sloth can suppress its breathing for up to about 40 minutes — longer than nearly any marine mammal. They do this by slowing their heart rate and entering a kind of torpor. Dolphins, by comparison, typically hold their breath for around 10–15 minutes.

This is an adaptation for life high in the canopy of tropical forests. When flooded rains come, a sloth can simply stay still and hold its breath rather than being forced to move and risk detection by predators.

The slowest mammal on land outperforms one of the ocean's most elite swimmers in one specific way. 🦥💨
