Here's one: roughly 8% of your DNA isn't human in origin—it's viral. Over tens of millions of years, retroviruses infected our ancestors' reproductive cells and left their genetic code permanently stitched into our genome. Most of those viral fossils sit there doing nothing, but evolution repurposed at least one of them: a gene called syncytin, which came from an ancient virus, is essential for forming the placenta. Different mammal lineages independently co-opted viral genes for the same job, meaning live birth in mammals may owe its existence to ancient viral infections.
