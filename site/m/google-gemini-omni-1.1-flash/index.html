Here is something about our own planet and solar system that blows most people's minds:

Sharks are older than trees. They are also older than Saturn's rings.

Here is the breakdown:

  • Sharks: The earliest evidence of shark scales dates back about 450 million years. They have survived four of the "Big Five" mass extinction events, including the one that wiped out the dinosaurs.
  • Trees: Plants with wood stems, leaves, and roots didn't evolve until about 350 million years ago. That means for roughly 100 million years, there was an Earth with sharks swimming in the oceans, but not a single tree on land.
  • Saturn's Rings: Recent data from the Cassini spacecraft suggests Saturn's rings are only 10 to 100 million years old (formed from a destroyed icy moon). That means sharks were swimming around on Earth for over 300 million years before Saturn even had rings.

If you already knew that, try these rapid-fire facts:

  1. Clouds are incredibly heavy: A typical, fluffy cumulus cloud weighs about 1.1 million pounds (500,000 kg). It stays afloat because that weight is spread out over a huge area, and the water droplets are resting on rising warm air.
  2. Sloths beat dolphins underwater: Sloths can hold their breath longer than dolphins. By slowing their heart rate down to a third of its normal pace, sloths can hold their breath underwater for up to 40 minutes. Dolphins usually need to surface for air after 10 to 15 minutes.
  3. Your head is older than your feet: According to Einstein’s theory of relativity, the closer you are to a center of gravity, the slower time moves. Because your feet are closer to the Earth than your head, time moves a fraction of a second slower for them. Over a 79-year lifespan, your head ends up about 90 billionths of a second older than your feet.
  4. The "immortal" jellyfish: There is a species of jellyfish (Turritopsis dohrnii) that, when it gets old, sick, or injured, can reverse its life cycle. It sinks to the ocean floor, turns back into a polyp (its baby state), and starts life all over again, effectively making it biologically immortal.
