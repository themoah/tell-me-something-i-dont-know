Here’s one: Wombats are the only known animals that naturally produce cube-shaped poop.

Most animals’ scat is round or oval because their intestines are basically uniform tubes. But wombats have intestines with regions of different stiffness—some parts are stretchy, others are rigid. As the poop moves through, those varying pressures mold it into flat-sided cubes, usually about 2 cm across.

Why? They live in rocky, arid parts of Australia and use their droppings to mark territory on boulders and logs. If the poop were round, it would roll away and lose its “message.” Cubes stay put.

Scientists at Georgia Tech only figured out the exact biomechanics behind this in recent years (they modeled the intestinal walls to see how the corners form). So unless you’ve read a soft-matter physics paper on marsupial digestion, you probably didn’t know that nature invented a biological 3D printer for poop.

If you want another, quick bonus: A day on Venus (243 Earth days) is longer than a year on Venus (225 Earth days). Because it rotates so slowly backward, the sun rises in the west and sets in the east—twice per orbit.
