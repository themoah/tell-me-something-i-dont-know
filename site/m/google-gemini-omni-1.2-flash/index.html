So, next time you look at a shark, remember: you are looking at a living lineage that is older than trees and older than the most famous feature of our solar system.

They are called Blaschko’s lines, and you have had them since you were an embryo.

In the late 19th century, a German dermatologist named Alfred Blaschko was studying patients with various skin conditions. He noticed that their rashes, moles, and pigmentations seemed to follow very specific, identical patterns across different patients.

He mapped them out and discovered that we all have a "map" written into our skin:

  • V-shapes over your upper back.
  • S-shapes on your abdomen and sides.
  • Inverted U-shapes from your breastbone to your upper arm.
  • Perpendicular waves down your arms and legs.

Why are they there? When you were just a tiny clump of cells in the womb, your cells multiplied and migrated to form your skin. Blaschko's lines are essentially the "footprints" of that cellular movement.

Why can't you see them? Normally, your skin cells all have the same pigment, so the lines are invisible. However, they can become visible under certain circumstances:

  1. Certain skin conditions: Some genetic disorders or rashes (like eczema or lupus) will follow these exact paths.
  2. Chimerism: If a person is a "chimera" (meaning they absorbed a twin in the womb and have two distinct sets of DNA), their skin cells might produce slightly different amounts of pigment, making the swirls and stripes clearly visible under normal light.
  3. UV Light: In some people, these lines can be seen under specific types of ultraviolet lighting.
